\section{Event decomposition of the three-phonon operator and energy-conserving stoichiometry}

\section{Lifetimes and dc response: sharpness of the bound $K_0\ge K_{\rm RTA}/\varsigma_{\max}$ and self-energy linewidths}

\paragraph*{Equality networks.}
By Lemma~2, the currents that attain $K_0=K_{\rm RTA}/\varsigma_{\max}$ are the vectors $\jj=\Cop\yy$ with
$\yy\ne0$ and $\Cop\yy=\varsigma_{\max}\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy$; this requires that every active
event touch exactly $\varsigma_{\max}$ modes and that the numbers $\zeta_{\gamma\nu}y_\nu$ be equal on the
support of each active event. Conversely, if both conditions hold for some $\yy\ne0$, with common value
$c_\gamma$ on $\mathrm{supp}\,\bm\zeta_\gamma$, then $\bm\zeta_\gamma\tr\yy=\varsigma_{\max}c_\gamma$ and
$\zeta_{\gamma\nu}^2y_\nu=\zeta_{\gamma\nu}c_\gamma$, so that
\begin{equation}
\Cop\yy=\sum_\gamma g_\gamma\,\bm\zeta_\gamma\,(\bm\zeta_\gamma\tr\yy)
=\varsigma_{\max}\sum_\gamma g_\gamma c_\gamma\,\bm\zeta_\gamma
=\varsigma_{\max}\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy ,
\label{eq:Seq}
\end{equation}
because $r_\nu y_\nu=\sum_\gamma g_\gamma\zeta_{\gamma\nu}^2y_\nu$. Then $\jj=\Cop\yy\in\Hsp\setminus\{0\}$,
$K_0=\yy\tr\Cop\yy=\varsigma_{\max}\,\yy\tr\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy$ and
$K_{\rm RTA}=\varsigma_{\max}^2\,\yy\tr\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy$, so that $K_0/K_{\rm RTA}=1/\varsigma_{\max}$
exactly.

For event vectors $\bm\zeta_\gamma=\mathsf W^{-1}\mathbf s_\gamma$ [Eq.~(15), derived there for three-phonon events; the same linearization gives $\bm\zeta_\gamma=\mathsf W^{-1}\mathbf s_\gamma$ for $1\leftrightarrow3$ and $2\leftrightarrow2$ events] and $\yy=\mathsf W\bm\psi$, one
has $\zeta_{\gamma\nu}y_\nu=s_{\gamma\nu}\psi_\nu$, so the conditions involve the stoichiometry only. If
every event of a network touches $\varsigma_{\max}$ modes and $s_{\gamma\nu}\psi_\nu$ takes a single value on
$\mathrm{supp}\,\mathbf s_\gamma$ for every $\gamma$, then Eq.~\eqref{eq:Seq} holds for all positive weights
$w_\nu$ and all positive rates $g_\gamma$, and the current $\jj=\Cop\,\mathsf W\bm\psi$ attains
$K_0/K_{\rm RTA}=1/\varsigma_{\max}$. Table~\ref{tab:sharp} lists three such networks. In each, the events
conserve energy ($\mathbf s_\gamma\tr\bm\varepsilon=0$) and the stoichiometric vectors have rank $n-1$; since
$\mathsf W$ is invertible, the $\bm\zeta_\gamma$ then span $\Hsp$, and every positive rate vector is
admissible. In the chain, $\psi_{\nu+1}=-2\psi_\nu$ gives $s_{\gamma\mu}\psi_\mu=2\psi_\nu$ on both modes
$\mu=\nu,\nu+1$ of the event $(\nu+1)\to\nu+\nu$. In the other two networks no mode is both a parent and a
daughter, and $\psi=+1$ on parents and $-1$ on daughters gives $s_{\gamma\nu}\psi_\nu=-1$ on every support;
the events of the network with $\varsigma_{\max}=4$ are $1\leftrightarrow3$ four-phonon processes. A
network of $2\leftrightarrow2$ events alone conserves the phonon number ($\mathbf s_\gamma\tr\mathbf 1=0$),
an invariant other than the energy unless all modes have the same energy, and is then not admissible.

\paragraph*{Verification.}
For each network of Table~\ref{tab:sharp}, the script \texttt{checks/\allowbreak verify\_manuscript\_algebra.py} of the
manuscript sources (block ``Lemma~2 sharpness'') checks in exact arithmetic the energy conservation, the
rank $n-1$, the identity $\Cop\yy=\varsigma_{\max}\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy$ for symbolic weights and
rates, and the ratio $K_0/K_{\rm RTA}=1/\varsigma_{\max}$ for rational weights and unequal rates, with
$K_0=\jj\tr(\Cop+\ee\ee\tr/|\ee|^2)^{-1}\jj$ computed without using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Seq}. The script
\texttt{checks/\allowbreak audit/\allowbreak audit\_lemma2.py} checks the same networks with other weights and rates.

\paragraph*{Networks with events of different sizes.}
By Lemma~2 the inequality is strict when an active event touches fewer than $\varsigma_{\max}$ modes; the
constant can nevertheless not be improved. Writing $\jj=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)^{1/2}\mathbf u$, where
$\mathbf u$ lies in the range of $G=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)^{-1/2}\,\Cop\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)^{-1/2}$
exactly when $\jj\in\Hsp$, gives $K_0=\mathbf u\tr G^+\mathbf u$ (if $G\mathbf z=\mathbf u$, then $\Cop\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)^{-1/2}\mathbf z=\jj$) and $K_{\rm RTA}=|\mathbf u|^2$. Hence
$\min_{\jj\in\Hsp\setminus\{0\}}K_0/K_{\rm RTA}=1/\lambda_{\max}(G)$, and $\lambda_{\max}(G)$ is at least
$\yy\tr\Cop\yy/\yy\tr\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy$ for every $\yy\ne0$. Take six modes with energies
$(1,2,3,4,5,6)$ and unit weights, the $2\leftrightarrow2$ event $2+5\to3+4$ with rate $1$, and the six
three-phonon events $3\to1+2$, $4\to1+3$, $5\to1+4$, $5\to2+3$, $6\to1+5$ and $6\to2+4$ with rate $\delta>0$.
Then $\varsigma_{\max}=4$, the stoichiometric rank is $5$, so that the rates are admissible, and
$\yy=(0,1,-1,-1,1,0)\tr$ gives $\yy\tr\Cop\yy=16+10\delta$ and $\yy\tr\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf r)\,\yy=4+12\delta$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
1<\min_{\jj\in\Hsp\setminus\{0\}}\frac{4K_0}{K_{\rm RTA}}\le\frac{16+48\delta}{16+10\delta}\to1
\qquad(\delta\to0).
\label{eq:mixed}
\end{equation}
The minima $4/\lambda_{\max}(G)$ are $1.2198$, $1.0234$, $1.00236$ and $1.000024$ for $\delta=10^{-1}$,
$10^{-2}$, $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-5}$ (script \texttt{checks/\allowbreak audit/\allowbreak audit\_lemma2.py}).

\paragraph*{Self-energy linewidths.}
Let $\tilde{\mathbf r}\le\mathbf r$ be a linewidth that is strictly smaller than $\mathbf r$ on at least one
mode carrying current, such as one that counts the self-coupling of a repeated daughter with half weight. At an
equality current $\jj=\Cop\yy$ of Lemma~2, $K_0=K_{\rm RTA}/\varsigma<\tilde K_{\rm RTA}/\varsigma$, so the
inequality fails for $\tilde{\mathbf r}$. For the chain with energies $1,2,4,8,16$ ($\varsigma=2$) and the
half-weight self-coupling, $K_0/(\tilde K_{\rm RTA}/2)=0.722$, $0.686$ and $0.688$ for three random sets of
weights and rates, in exact arithmetic (\texttt{checks/\allowbreak audit\_supp/\allowbreak audit\_s2\_networks.py}).

\section{Lifetime-constrained sharpness of the cone bound: an exact seven-mode example}

\section{Infrared criterion: lattice sums and mesh laws}

This section proves the mesh laws of Theorem~3(i) and the lower bound used for Theorem~3(ii); the
integral criterion is proved in the main text. The meshes are $\mathbf k=\mathsf B\mathbf n/N_1$ with
$\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z^d$, where the columns of $\mathsf B$ are primitive reciprocal-lattice vectors, so that
$|\det\mathsf B|\,V_{\rm cell}=(2\pi)^d$ and $\mathcal V=N_1^dV_{\rm cell}$. The radius $k_0$ is taken so
small that the ball $k<k_0$ lies inside the Brillouin zone and the $o(1)$ of (H1) is at most $1/2$ there.
As in (H2) and (H5), $\Phi_m=\sum_{\rm ac}J_0^2r_0^{-m}$ and $A_m=\int_{S^{d-1}}\Phi_m\,d\Omega$; the mesh
moments are $\mathcal M_m(N_1)=\mathcal V^{-1}\sum_\nu J(\mathbf k_\nu,s_\nu)^2r_\nu^{-m}$.

\begin{lemma}[Lattice sums]\label{lem:lattice}
Let $\mathsf B\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ be invertible, $m\ge0$ and $\alpha>0$, and let
$\Phi\colon S^{d-1}\to[0,\infty)$ be bounded and continuous outside a closed set of zero measure, with
$A=\int_{S^{d-1}}\Phi\,d\Omega>0$. For $\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z^d\setminus\{0\}$ put
$\varphi(\mathbf n)=\Phi(\mathsf B\mathbf n/|\mathsf B\mathbf n|)\,|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}$. Let $k_0>0$,
let $\vartheta$ be a bounded function on $(0,k_0)$ with $\vartheta(t)\to0$ as $t\to0$, and for
$N\in\mathbb N$ let $\epsilon_N(\mathbf n)$ be real numbers with
$|\epsilon_N(\mathbf n)|\le\vartheta(|\mathsf B\mathbf n|/N)$. Put
\begin{equation}
S_N=\sum_{0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<k_0N}\big[\varphi(\mathbf n)+\epsilon_N(\mathbf n)\,|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}\big],
\qquad\bar\vartheta=\sup_{0<t<k_0}\vartheta(t).
\label{eq:SN}
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $m\alpha<d$, there is $C$, depending only on $\mathsf B$, $d$ and $m\alpha$, such that
$N^{m\alpha-d}\sum_{0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<\delta N}|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}\le C\delta^{d-m\alpha}$ for
all $N$ and all $\delta>0$, and
\begin{equation}
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\,\Big|N^{m\alpha-d}S_N-\frac{A\,k_0^{d-m\alpha}}{(d-m\alpha)\,|\det\mathsf B|}\Big|
\le\frac{\bar\vartheta\,|S^{d-1}|\,k_0^{d-m\alpha}}{(d-m\alpha)\,|\det\mathsf B|}.
\label{eq:Sa}
\end{equation}
\item[(b)] If $m\alpha=d$, then $S_N=|\det\mathsf B|^{-1}A\log N+o(\log N)$.
\item[(c)] If $m\alpha>d$, then $S_N\to S_\Phi:=\sum_{\mathbf n\ne0}\varphi(\mathbf n)$, and $0<S_\Phi<\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Counting.} Let $\rho_0=\min_{\mathbf n\ne0}|\mathsf B\mathbf n|>0$. The balls of radius $\rho_0/2$
centred at the points $\mathsf B\mathbf n$ are disjoint, so for $R\ge\rho_0$ at most $(3R/\rho_0)^d$ of these
points satisfy $0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<R$. The dyadic shell
$\Sigma_l=\{\mathbf n:2^l\le|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<2^{l+1}\}$ ($l\in\mathbb Z$) is empty unless
$2^{l+1}>\rho_0$, and then
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\mathbf n\in\Sigma_l}|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}\le C_1\,2^{l(d-m\alpha)},\qquad C_1=(6/\rho_0)^d .
\label{eq:shellbound}
\end{equation}

\emph{Shell Riemann sums.} Let $D=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^d:1\le|\mathsf B\mathbf u|<2\}$ and
$\tilde\varphi(\mathbf u)=\Phi(\mathsf B\mathbf u/|\mathsf B\mathbf u|)\,|\mathsf B\mathbf u|^{-m\alpha}$. On
$D$, $\tilde\varphi$ is bounded and continuous outside a set of zero measure (a cone over the
discontinuity set of $\Phi$), and the boundary of $D$, two ellipsoids, has zero measure. By Lebesgue's
criterion $\tilde\varphi$ is Riemann integrable on $D$, so the lattice sums
$h^d\sum_{\mathbf u\in h\mathbb Z^d\cap D}\tilde\varphi(\mathbf u)$, which are Riemann sums for the
partition of space into the cubes $\mathbf u+[0,h)^d$, converge as $h\to0$ to
$c_\ast=\int_D\tilde\varphi\,d\mathbf u=|\det\mathsf B|^{-1}A\int_1^2\rho^{d-1-m\alpha}d\rho>0$; the last form
follows from the substitution $\mathbf v=\mathsf B\mathbf u$ and polar coordinates. Since
$\varphi(\mathbf n)=2^{-lm\alpha}\tilde\varphi(2^{-l}\mathbf n)$, and $\mathbf n\in\Sigma_l$ if and only if
$2^{-l}\mathbf n\in D$,
\begin{equation}
a_l:=\sum_{\mathbf n\in\Sigma_l}\varphi(\mathbf n)
=2^{l(d-m\alpha)}\Big[2^{-ld}\!\!\sum_{\mathbf u\in2^{-l}\mathbb Z^d\cap D}\!\!\tilde\varphi(\mathbf u)\Big]
=2^{l(d-m\alpha)}\,[c_\ast+o(1)]\qquad(l\to\infty).
\label{eq:shellsum}
\end{equation}

\emph{Part (a).} Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:shellbound} over the shells with $2^l<R$ gives
$\sum_{0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<R}|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}\le C_1R^{d-m\alpha}/(1-2^{m\alpha-d})$ for every
$R>0$, which is the first claim with $R=\delta N$. For $0<\delta<k_0$,
$N^{m\alpha-d}\sum_{\delta N\le|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<k_0N}\varphi(\mathbf n)
=N^{-d}\sum_{\mathbf u\in N^{-1}\mathbb Z^d,\,\delta\le|\mathsf B\mathbf u|<k_0}\tilde\varphi(\mathbf u)$ is a Riemann
sum of $\tilde\varphi$ over $\{\delta\le|\mathsf B\mathbf u|<k_0\}$, on which $\tilde\varphi$ is Riemann
integrable as above; it converges to
$|\det\mathsf B|^{-1}A\,(k_0^{d-m\alpha}-\delta^{d-m\alpha})/(d-m\alpha)$. The points with
$0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<\delta N$ contribute at most $C\delta^{d-m\alpha}\sup\Phi$. Letting $N\to\infty$ and
then $\delta\to0$ gives the limit of $N^{m\alpha-d}\sum_{0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<k_0N}\varphi(\mathbf n)$
displayed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sa}. The same argument with $\Phi\equiv1$ gives the limit of
$N^{m\alpha-d}\sum_{0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<k_0N}|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}$, and the error terms are at
most $\bar\vartheta$ times this sum.

\emph{Part (b).} Now $2^{l(d-m\alpha)}=1$ and $a_l\to c_\ast=|\det\mathsf B|^{-1}A\log2$. Let $L_N$ be the
integer with $2^{L_N}\le k_0N<2^{L_N+1}$. The points with $0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<2^{L_N}$ contribute
$\sum_{l<L_N}a_l=c_\ast L_N+o(L_N)$, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:shellsum} and because only finitely many shells with
$l<0$ are nonempty; the remaining points lie in $\Sigma_{L_N}$ and contribute at most $C_1\sup\Phi$. Since
$L_N\log2=\log N+O(1)$, the sum of $\varphi$ is $|\det\mathsf B|^{-1}A\log N+o(\log N)$. For the error
terms, given $\epsilon'>0$ choose $\delta\in(0,k_0)$ with $\vartheta(t)\le\epsilon'$ for $t<\delta$. By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:shellbound}, the points with $|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<\delta N$ contribute at most $\epsilon'C_1$
times the number of nonempty shells below $\delta N$, which is $\log_2N+O(1)$, and the points with
$\delta N\le|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<k_0N$ at most $\bar\vartheta C_1[\log_2(k_0/\delta)+2]$, which does not depend
on $N$. Since $\epsilon'$ is arbitrary, the error terms are $o(\log N)$.

\emph{Part (c).} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:shellbound}, $\sum_{\mathbf n\ne0}|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}\le
C_1\sum_{l:\,2^{l+1}>\rho_0}2^{l(d-m\alpha)}<\infty$, so $S_\Phi$ converges and the truncated sums of
$\varphi$ converge to it. The error terms are dominated by $\bar\vartheta\,|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-m\alpha}$ and
tend to zero for every fixed $\mathbf n$ as $N\to\infty$, because $|\mathsf B\mathbf n|/N\to0$; by dominated
convergence their sum tends to zero. Finally, $\{\Phi>0\}$ has positive measure and the discontinuity set
of $\Phi$ has zero measure, so $\Phi$ is continuous and positive at some direction and positive on a
neighbourhood $U$ of it. The directions $\mathsf B\mathbf n/|\mathsf B\mathbf n|$ are dense in $S^{d-1}$,
being the image of the rational directions under a homeomorphism of the sphere; some $\mathbf n$ has
$\mathsf B\mathbf n/|\mathsf B\mathbf n|\in U$, and then $\varphi(\mathbf n)>0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph*{Regularity is necessary.}
Let $d\ge2$. If $\Phi=0$ on the countable set of directions $\mathsf B\mathbf n/|\mathsf B\mathbf n|$ and $\Phi=1$
elsewhere, then $A=|S^{d-1}|>0$ but $\varphi\equiv0$, and $S_N$ consists of the error terms alone. Taking
$J=J_0(\hat{\mathbf k})$ with such a $J_0^2$ and $r=k^\alpha$ near $\Gamma$ satisfies (H1)--(H3), but every
mesh point near $\Gamma$ then has $J=0$ and the mesh laws fail. Likewise, setting $J=0$ at the mesh points
with $k\ge k_0$ of all meshes, a countable set, changes neither (H1)--(H3) nor any $\mathcal M_m$, but
removes the part $k\ge k_0$ of every mesh sum. Hypothesis (H5) excludes both constructions and admits the
discontinuities of frequency-sorted acoustic branches, which lie on a closed set of directions of zero
measure (acoustic axes, where branches touch conically and the group velocity of a sorted branch depends
on the direction of approach, and curves where branches cross).

\paragraph*{Mesh laws.}
Assume (H1)--(H3) and (H5). For $k<k_0$, (H1)--(H2) give
$\sum_{\rm ac}J^2r^{-m}=[\Phi_m(\hat{\mathbf k})+\epsilon_{\rm ac}(\mathbf k)]\,k^{-m\alpha}$ with
$\sup_{\hat{\mathbf k}}|\epsilon_{\rm ac}(\mathbf k)|\to0$ as $k\to0$; the error is additive because $J_0$
may vanish on a plane. At $\mathbf k=\mathsf B\mathbf n/N_1$ the acoustic part $k_\nu<k_0$ of
$\mathcal M_m(N_1)$ is therefore $N_1^{m\alpha-d}V_{\rm cell}^{-1}S_{N_1}$, with $S_N$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:SN}
for $\Phi=\Phi_m$ and $\epsilon_N(\mathbf n)=\epsilon_{\rm ac}(\mathsf B\mathbf n/N)$, while the optical branches
and the part $k\ge k_0$ contribute a bounded amount, by (H3) and the boundedness of $J$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:lattice} and $|\det\mathsf B|\,V_{\rm cell}=(2\pi)^d$ give:
\begin{itemize}
\item $m\alpha>d$: $\mathcal M_m(N_1)=N_1^{m\alpha-d}S_m/V_{\rm cell}\,[1+o(1)]$, with $S_m=S_{\Phi_m}\in(0,\infty)$;
\item $m\alpha=d$: $\mathcal M_m(N_1)=(2\pi)^{-d}A_m\log N_1+o(\log N_1)$;
\item $m\alpha<d$: for $0<\delta<k_0$ the part $k<\delta$ of $\mathcal M_m(N_1)$ is at most
$C'\delta^{d-m\alpha}$ for all $N_1$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:lattice}(a) for the acoustic branches and by the same lemma with $m=0$ for the optical branches (at most $C''\delta^d$), and so is the part $k<\delta$ of
$\mathcal M_m$. On $k\ge\delta$ the integrands are bounded, by (H1) and (H3), and Riemann integrable, by
(H5), so the remaining mesh sums converge to the remaining part of $\mathcal M_m$. Hence
$\limsup_{N_1\to\infty}|\mathcal M_m(N_1)-\mathcal M_m|\le2C'\delta^{d-m\alpha}$ for every $\delta$, that is,
$\mathcal M_m(N_1)\to\mathcal M_m$.
\end{itemize}
For $\tmem^{\rm RTA}=\mathcal M_2(N_1)/\mathcal M_1(N_1)$ on the meshes this gives:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2\alpha<d$: $\tmem^{\rm RTA}\to\mathcal M_2/\mathcal M_1$;
\item $2\alpha=d$: $\tmem^{\rm RTA}=(2\pi)^{-d}A_2\log N_1/\mathcal M_1\,[1+o(1)]$;
\item $d/2<\alpha<d$: $\tmem^{\rm RTA}=N_1^{2\alpha-d}S_2/(V_{\rm cell}\mathcal M_1)\,[1+o(1)]$;
\item $\alpha=d$: $\tmem^{\rm RTA}=(2\pi)^dS_2N_1^d/(V_{\rm cell}A_1\log N_1)\,[1+o(1)]$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph*{Event operator.}
Under the uniform (H1) of Theorem~3(ii) there is $\delta_1(k)\to0$, independent of $N_1$, with
$r_\nu\le r_0(\hat{\mathbf k}_\nu)k_\nu^\alpha[1+\delta_1(k_\nu)]$ for all $N_1$ and all acoustic modes with
$k_\nu<k_0$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\sum_{{\rm ac},\,k_\nu<k_0}j_\nu^2r_\nu^{-2}\ \ge\ \frac{N_1^{2\alpha-d}}{V_{\rm cell}}
\sum_{0<|\mathsf B\mathbf n|<k_0N_1}\big[\varphi(\mathbf n)+\epsilon'(\mathsf B\mathbf n/N_1)\,|\mathsf B\mathbf n|^{-2\alpha}\big],
\end{equation}
where $\varphi$ is built from $\Phi_2$ and
$\epsilon'(\mathbf k)=\sum_{\rm ac}J^2r_0^{-2}[1+\delta_1(k)]^{-2}-\Phi_2(\hat{\mathbf k})$ depends on $\mathbf k$
only and tends to zero uniformly in $\hat{\mathbf k}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:lattice}(b) and (c) the right
side equals $(2\pi)^{-d}A_2\log N_1\,[1+o(1)]$ for $2\alpha=d$ and
$N_1^{2\alpha-d}S_2/V_{\rm cell}\,[1+o(1)]$ for $2\alpha>d$. Only the continuity of $\Phi_2$ outside a
closed set of zero measure enters, not the Riemann integrability of $J^2r^{-2}$ away from $\Gamma$, which
for $\mathbf r=\mathrm{diag}\,\Cop_{N_1}$ would depend on $N_1$. With (H4) and Proposition~2 this proves
Theorem~3(ii).

\paragraph*{Numerical check.}
The script \texttt{checks/\allowbreak audit/\allowbreak audit\_ir\_lattice.py} of the manuscript sources
compares the constants with direct lattice sums (double precision; continuum moments and $A_m$ by adaptive
quadrature; $S_2$ by a lattice sum over $|\mathbf n|\le1500$ with a continuum tail).
The test uses $d=2$, $\mathsf B=2\pi I$ ($V_{\rm cell}=1$), odd $N_1$ from $101$ to $3201$,
$J^2=(\hat k_x^2+0.3k)\,e^{-k^2/8}$ and $r=(1+2.4\hat k_x^2\hat k_y^2)\,k^\alpha(1+0.5k^2)$. Here
$J_0^2=\hat k_x^2$ vanishes on the line $\hat k_x=0$, so the correction to $J^2$ is additive.
Table~\ref{tab:ir} gives the results at $N_1=3201$. The laws are approached slowly. Through
$\mathcal M_1(N_1)$, which in this test converges like $N_1^{-(d-\alpha)}$, the ratio of
$\tmem^{\rm RTA}$ to $N_1^{2\alpha-d}S_2/(V_{\rm cell}\mathcal M_1)$ is still $1.026$ at $N_1=3201$ for
$\alpha=1.5$, and at $\alpha=d$ the relative correction decays like $1/\log N_1$. Exponents fitted on
